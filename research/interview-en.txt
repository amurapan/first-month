DISCOVERY INTERVIEW — draft, not yet run
20 minutes. Show the concept only after the past-behavior questions.

Recruitment text, ready to adapt when a permitted channel is available:
We are a hackathon team exploring how couples organize shared household
expenses when they first move in together. We would like to hear about what
you actually did and try a short paper exercise. We are looking for adults
aged 25–45 living in the US who began living with a romantic partner in the
past 12 months. Participation is voluntary and unpaid. You can skip any
question. Approximate figures are enough; no statements or account access
are needed. We are equally interested in arrangements that work well.

SCREENING — ask before inviting to a session
1. What country do you currently live in?
2. Which age band applies: under 25 / 25–34 / 35–45 / 46+?
3. Which describes your current situation: living with a romantic partner /
   planning to move in / living separately without a move planned?
4. When did you start living together? Was this your first time living
   with this partner? Approximate month/year is enough.
5. Are you personally involved in arranging or paying shared expenses?
6. Are you willing to discuss your experience for 20 minutes? Your partner
   need not attend the individual interview. A joint test requires both
   people's separate agreement.

Do not select only people who report financial conflict. Record planning
couples separately from people who have already moved in. Target three to
five households initially, not a statistical estimate of the market.

OPENING
We are testing our assumptions, not your relationship or financial choices.
There are no right answers. You can stop or skip a topic. May I take brief
anonymous notes? Do not record audio/video unless separately agreed.

BEFORE SHOWING ANY CONCEPT — about 12 minutes
1. Walk me through how you decided who would pay for what when you moved in.
2. What did you explicitly agree on before the move? What remained open?
3. Walk me through the most recent shared purchase or bill, from deciding
   to buy/pay it through settling up. Who did what?
4. What, if anything, surprised you about shared expenses after moving in?
   Tell me about one specific occasion. What happened next?
5. Have you changed your arrangement? What triggered that change, and how
   did you reach the new arrangement? If nothing changed, what works well?
6. What do you currently use to manage this? What have you tried or paid
   for? How much time or effort does your current approach take?
7. Is there anything you wish you had discussed earlier? Why then? Do not
   supply a list of categories unless the participant asks for examples.

Neutral follow-ups: When was that? What happened next? How did you decide?
Who took the next step? Can you give a specific example?
Avoid: Would this app help? Would you pay $10? Don't you find this stressful?

CONCEPT EXERCISE — about 6 minutes
Show a blank worksheet.txt. Say:
This is a draft tool for agreeing on one month of shared expenses. Imagine
using it at the point you described. Which part, if any, would have changed
what you actually did? Which part would be unnecessary? What is missing?

If comfortable, ask them to work through the relevant section using rounded
or fictional values. Observe actions before asking for opinions.
Ask: What would you do with this result next? How does it compare with the
way you already handle this? It is fine to prefer your current approach.

CLOSE — about 2 minutes
Ask which decision, if any, remains unresolved. Ask permission before a
future follow-up or inviting their partner. Do not count stated enthusiasm
as purchase intent or evidence of retention.

Keep records separate: past behavior / observed task action / opinion /
researcher interpretation. Do not put real financial details or identifiers
in a public hackathon repository. Record approximate figures only if needed.
